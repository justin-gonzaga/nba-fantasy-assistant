## What this means for ANL-010

**Effects worth testing, with expected magnitudes (sourced):**

1. **Pace rescaling is mechanical and large, and must be applied before testing any other team effect.** NBA
   team pace varies enough that cross-league possession counts differ by ~130% (R-110); within-NBA team pace
   differences are smaller but not trivial season to season. Any "did the team change this player's counting
   stats" test must first convert to a per-possession/per-100 basis, or pace alone will manufacture a false
   positive "team effect" on raw totals.
2. **A coach's playing-time/rotation philosophy has a real but modest, team-level effect** (R-111: ~1–2 wins
   per season per SD of playing-time-concentration change, controlling for talent) and **a coach's in-game
   scheme contribution is usually smaller than reputation suggests** — most "great coach" records are driven
   by roster quality, with only a minority of coaches showing a large independent scheme effect (R-112). Size
   any individual-level "coaching-change effect" test's expected effect accordingly: look for a modest shift
   in rotation depth / minutes distribution, not a large one, and do not assume every coach matters equally.
3. **All-in-one teammate-adjusted ratings (APM/RPM-style) are not safely portable across a team change.**
   R-113's IV estimate (~0.664 RPM points gained per point of average teammate-RPM improvement) is the
   clearest quantitative anchor in this file for "how much of an individual rating is really team context" —
   if ANL-010 ever uses such a rating as a feature or target, this result argues for explicitly modelling a
   team/teammate random effect alongside a player effect (R-113, R-114-style decomposition), rather than
   assuming the rating travels with the player.
4. **The contract-cycle confound is sizeable (β≈0.68 MP48 vs. mean 8.24, ~8%: R-118, corroborated in direction
   by peer-reviewed R-116/R-117) and will contaminate any naive before/after-trade comparison** unless
   contract-year timing is controlled for separately from the team-change indicator.

**Confounders each design must handle:**

- **Selection into trades/coaching changes**: teams do not trade players at random — players are traded when
  a team believes their value has peaked, declined, or no longer fits; R-112 shows even a team's own coaching
  "quality" signal is mostly a roster-quality artefact, reinforcing that observed post-move performance change
  reflects who gets moved, not only what the move does. A movers design (R-114) or synthetic control (R-115)
  must condition on pre-move trend, not just pre-move level, to separate this from a true team effect.
- **Regression to the mean**: players are frequently traded or sign new contracts coming off an outlier
  (good or bad) season; R-116/R-117/R-118 show individual production already has non-team-related, cyclical
  swings (contract-year effort) that look superficially like "team effects" if not controlled for.
- **Roster turnover entangled with coaching changes**: a new head coach is very often accompanied by, or
  followed by, substantial roster turnover (R-117's own control variable, ΔROSTER, exists precisely because
  the authors could not otherwise separate coach effects from teammate-quality effects). A design that
  attributes a player's production change to "new coach" without also controlling for "new teammates" (e.g.
  via the two-way fixed-effects / movers approach in R-114) will misattribute the effect.
- **Metric sensitivity**: R-117 shows the presence/absence of an effect can flip depending on which
  productivity metric is used (box-score efficiency vs. marginal-product measure) — any ANL-010 test should
  check robustness across at least two different outcome metrics before concluding an effect is real.

**Recommended design pattern for ANL-010** (not itself a citable finding — a synthesis): a player/team(+coach)
two-way fixed-effects ("movers design," R-114) model on our own panel, with contract-year/post-contract
dummies (R-116–R-118) and a roster-turnover control (as in R-117) included explicitly, run on pace-adjusted
per-possession rate stats (R-110); for individual high-profile moves with too few comparable "movers" for
fixed effects to be well-identified, fall back to a synthetic-control comparison (R-115) against a donor pool
of similar players who did not change teams that season.

## Gaps (no peer-reviewed support found this pass)

- **Q1 — is pace a team trait or a coach trait?** R-110 establishes that pace materially scales counting
  stats and varies across teams/leagues, but does not decompose how much of a team's pace is attributable to
  the head coach specifically (as opposed to roster personnel, front-office philosophy, or opponent pace). No
  peer-reviewed source answering this decomposition was found; this is an empirical question for our own data
  (e.g. does a team's pace rank move with a coach when the coach changes teams, holding roster constant?).
- **Q2 — the empirical split of vacated usage/shots/assists among remaining teammates.** As already logged in
  `lit-breakouts.md` (R-87's gap), no peer-reviewed paper measuring the *actual* NBA redistribution split
  (which teammates absorb how much of a departed player's usage, on average) was found this pass either; R-87
  (theory, non-proportional/efficiency-weighted redistribution) remains the closest peer-reviewed support.
  Treat the redistribution rule as a hypothesis to estimate on our own data, not an established finding.
- **Q3 — a peer-reviewed study of a coaching change's effect on an individual player's minutes, usage, 3PA
  rate, or rebounding specifically** (as opposed to team win% in R-86, or team-level rotation-concentration in
  R-111). None was found. R-111 and R-112 are the closest, both team-outcome-level. Whether a given coach's
  tendencies (pace, 3PA rate, rotation depth) persist when that coach moves to a new team was not found as a
  peer-reviewed individual-level result either.
- **Q6 — a sports-specific (ideally NBA-specific) application of the movers design or synthetic control to a
  player trade or coaching change.** R-114 and R-115 are general/cross-domain methodological sources (labour
  economics, policy evaluation); no NBA-specific peer-reviewed paper applying either method to a trade or
  coaching-change event was found this pass. The "limited mobility bias" caveat on movers designs (noted in
  the general AKM literature during this pass's search, but not independently verified against a primary
  source) should be checked against our own panel's mover-count-per-team-season before relying on it.
- **Q5 — a peer-reviewed, NBA-specific magnitude for post-trade production change vs. regression to the
  mean** (as opposed to the contract-cycle magnitude in R-116–R-118, which is a different event). R-85 in
  `lit-breakouts.md` (Campbell, Saxton & Banerjee 2014, comobility) is the closest peer-reviewed NBA-specific
  trade-performance result, but gives a qualitative/directional effect (movers show an adverse shock,
  moderated by group mobility) without the quantitative magnitude needed to size an expectation; a
  "PER dropped ~2.2%, OWS/WS/VORP declined 5–10%" figure found in search results during this pass (from a
  Skidmore College student economics paper on 2015–16 trades) could not be independently verified this pass
  (the source URL could not be resolved/fetched) and is **not included above** — do not cite it until it has
  been located and verified.
