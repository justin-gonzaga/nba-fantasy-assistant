## Gaps (no peer-reviewed support)

- **Topic 1 (a dedicated minutes-played / discrete role-change forecasting model):** no peer-reviewed paper
  was found that directly forecasts NBA minutes played or classifies season-to-season role changes (e.g.,
  bench → starter) as its primary object. R-84 is the closest peer-reviewed hit — it *conditions* production
  curves on minutes/usage/position — but it does not itself predict minutes. This mirrors the existing gap
  already logged in `ml-literature-review.md` §8 ("Minutes projection in basketball: the academic literature
  is thin"). Practitioner minutes-projection systems exist but are not peer-reviewed and were not added here.
- **Topic 2 (a dedicated NBA "breakout season" classifier/predictor):** no NBA-specific peer-reviewed paper
  defining or predicting a discrete "breakout" event was found (only a public, non-peer-reviewed GitHub
  project). R-88 (soccer, sophomore slump) and R-84 (production curves) are the closest peer-reviewed
  analogues, both requiring caveats (cross-sport for R-88; not breakout-specific for R-84). The practitioner
  concept of "per-minute efficiency at low minutes as a signal of latent talent" (used already in the
  draft-slice stabilization discussion, R-75/R-76/R-77) has no dedicated peer-reviewed NBA breakout study
  behind it; treat any breakout-labelling rule as a hypothesis to validate empirically on our own data
  (RSCH-005/RSCH-006 pattern), not as an established finding.
- **Topic 3 (an individual player's minutes shift caused specifically by a coaching change):** R-86 is
  peer-reviewed but measures *team* win-percentage outcomes of a coaching change, not an individual player's
  minutes. No peer-reviewed source measuring the individual-level minutes effect of a coaching change was
  found; this should be estimated empirically from our own data (event-study style, coaching-change date as
  the treatment).
- **Topic 3 (the empirical split of vacated usage/minutes among remaining teammates):** R-87 gives a
  theoretical argument for non-proportional (efficiency-weighted) redistribution, but no peer-reviewed paper
  measuring the *actual* NBA redistribution split (e.g., what fraction of a departed starter's minutes goes
  to which teammate, on average) was found; this is an empirical estimation task for our own data, not an
  academic result to cite.
- **Topic 4 (precision@k / calibration applied specifically to a sports "breakout" or rare-outcome task):**
  R-89 and R-90 are general (imbalanced learning; IR ranking evaluation), and R-40–R-48 already cover proper
  scoring rules and calibration generally; no peer-reviewed paper applying precision@k or calibration
  evaluation specifically to a sports breakout-prediction or top-k rare-talent-identification task was found.
  The general frameworks (R-40–R-48, R-89, R-90) should be adapted to our own breakout/top-k evaluation
  rather than ported from a sport-specific precedent.
