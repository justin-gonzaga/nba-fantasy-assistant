## Flags for the plan

- **R-15 (Sill 2010)**: Access was limited to secondary descriptions (the PDF mirror could not be parsed by
  the fetch tool); recommend a human open the PDF directly to confirm no further nuance is lost, and note in
  the ML plan that this is a conference paper (MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference), not a peer-reviewed
  journal article. It supports lineup-dummy/RAPM-style features but is a shrinkage estimator of individual
  impact rather than an explicit pairwise-interaction model — the design doc should not overstate it as
  "interaction modelling."
- **R-16 (Hägglund et al. 2006)**: Rated **Partially supports**. It is a football (soccer), not basketball,
  cohort; the "previous injury predicts future injury" direction is well evidenced cross-sport, but magnitude
  and mechanism transfer to the NBA is unverified. This is already flagged in `ml-literature-review.md` §8 as
  a known gap for RSCH-002 — Part A confirms the flag should stay and additionally suggests RSCH-004 record
  it as a claim needing our own NBA-data validation before injury-history features are weighted using
  soccer-derived effect sizes.
- **R-04 (Hunter, Vielma & Zaman 2016)**: The paper's constraint example is NHL positions, not NBA; our use
  requires adapting the position-constraint block ourselves. Not a source problem, just a note for whoever
  implements the MILP that the paper doesn't hand us NBA-specific constraints.
- **Access-limited entries** (abstract only, not full text): R-11, R-14, R-15, R-20, R-21, R-22, R-24, R-30,
  R-31. None of these raised a correction or a "does not support" rating, but if any of these claims becomes
  load-bearing for a gated decision, a follow-up should fetch full text (e.g. via institutional access) rather
  than relying on abstracts/secondary descriptions.
